## Algebra and directional scope

For the live map A=I−σJ and v=f−Ax0, direct expansion gives

A[(1−aσ)v+σJv] = (1−aσ−bσ²)v − σ²(J²v−aJv−bv).

Thus D≠0 implies f−A(x0+q)=σ²e_v/D. The sign, warm-start dependence, and use of the raw full candidate are correct. The native form `(2−aσ)v−Av` is algebraically identical and needs no division by σ. The relation is required on the current v only; it cannot be inferred for another stage or operator. At σ=0 the formula gives q=v. A denominator refusal is a representation refusal, not a singularity theorem. These conclusions require linearity of the declared A; arbitrary nonlinear or stateful callbacks are not covered merely because they implement an operator interface.

The original residual gate independently validates an acceptable current candidate even when the caller supplies incorrect coefficients or a mismatched σ. It does not validate the hypothesized polynomial identity. Nonfinite/incomplete callback output and actual callback errors remain different from ordinary proposal refusal. The documented initial-convergence shortcut correctly uses A0=0 for a linear map and explicitly discloses its different diagnostic callback count.

## Concrete dynamical-system structure

For the actual rotation R=[[0,−3],[3,0]], R²=−9I and Rᵀ=−R. Hence D=1+9σ² and AᵀA=D I. The Euclidean resolvent norm is exactly D^(−1/2); μ₂(R)=0. This is an operator-norm proof and does not rely on eigenvalue containment for a nonnormal matrix.

For the benchmark damped rotation J=−λI+R with λ=1/2, expansion yields J²+2λJ+(λ²+9)I=0, so the implemented a=−1 and b=−37/4 are correct. Here D=(1+λσ)²+9σ², AᵀA=D I, and μ₂(J)=−λ. For nonnegative σ this is uniformly nonsingular. The same directional proposal therefore removes Arnoldi/MGS/QR construction under this exact represented model, subject to its live floating residual gate.

For the actual nonautonomous time-JVP workload, J is diagonal with λ_i=−(2+i/8). The implemented polynomial is p(z)=(z+2)(z+31/8), and D=(1+2σ)(1+31σ/8). Its interval width is15/8; completing the square proves max |p(λ)|≤(15/8)²/4=225/256. The finite16-point grid improves this to7/8, as stated. Because this J is diagonal, ||p(J)||₂=max_i|p(λ_i)|; that step is invalid for a general nonnormal J and the document correctly excludes that extension. A v supported on more than two distinct eigendirections cannot satisfy p(J)v=0. For σ≥0, ||A⁻¹||₂=1/(1+2σ), yielding the stated residual and forward-error inequalities. Small σ may allow an approximate relation to pass the unchanged absolute/relative threshold; success there is not evidence of an exact quadratic annihilator.
