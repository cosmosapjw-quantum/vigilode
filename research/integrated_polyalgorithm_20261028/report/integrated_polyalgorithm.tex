\begin{abstract}
We consolidate the numerical implementations and mathematical constructions of
PR90--93 into a target-preserving solver design. The principal new connections
are a selection-independent physical error induction with two distinct valid
anchors, an output-specific quotient compiler for complete affine raw-$U$ stage
systems, and a precision-aware near-inverse correction rule. The compiler
reduces a requested endpoint to one polynomial of degree below the declared
operator-relation degree, while an explicit original-target residual extends
the construction to near structure, stored coefficients, and bounded nonlinear
remainders. A finite-menu error allocator and complete-session continuation
contract connect these local methods to the adapter. Two source-registered
exact-rational programs pass nine compiler fixtures and six physical-error
check groups, together with correction and refusal controls. These checks
support the written derivations; they do not validate new binary64 kernels or
measure native performance. The report also specifies how a separate PR can
include and validate all four preceding PRs without waiting for their merger.
Its coverage records 73 changed crate/workflow paths, preserves seven unresolved
qualification tasks and prior negative controls, and distinguishes source
inclusion, scientific verification, and release readiness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scientific question and cumulative source}

The goal is to exploit VigilODE's polyalgorithm: retain several faithful
realizations of the same numerical target, remove work when declared structure
permits it, and select an admitted candidate under a common accuracy and cost
contract. The intended character is derivative-free first, with explicitly
supplied JVP/AD capabilities used selectively where justified. An action can
be matrix-free and still use a derivative; neither a polynomial basis nor a
callback name establishes derivative freedom.

The four source PRs retain their own reports and receipts\cite{pr90,pr91,pr92,pr93}.

\section{One target, several admissible realizations}

Each request fixes its model and provider epoch, input state and time,
observations, norm, domain, arithmetic, callback permissions, and mathematical
target. Four targets must remain distinguishable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an original shifted linear system, checked by its original residual;
\item a represented fused $\varphi$ combination, checked by complete action error;
\item an original nonlinear stage/root, checked by residual, domain and branch;
\item a physical step, checked by temporal, algebraic, representation and clock
      errors under a valid propagation argument.
\end{enumerate}
A polynomial-action admission is a useful leaf of a physical-step certificate,
but it is not the latter. Similarly, a quotient-stage endpoint below has the
same discrete Rosenbrock target while still needing a temporal-error argument.
Private adapters around existing result types can enforce these connections;
the synthesis does not require replacing the solver hierarchy with a new
general certificate framework.

\subsection{Physical induction independent of selector smoothness}

Let the original ODE have a unique flow $\Phi(t,s,x)$ on a certified tube.
Use one physical norm $\norm{\cdot}_*$. The accepted times
$t_0<\cdots<t_N$ may depend on the computed history. Rejected attempts do
not advance the physical state. At the realized accepted step, assume
\begin{align}
 \norm{\Phi(t_{n+1},t_n,x)-\Phi(t_{n+1},t_n,z)}_*
   &\le \Lambda_n\norm{x-z}_*,\label{eq:flowstab}\\
 \norm{\widehat y_{n+1}-\Phi(t_{n+1},t_n,\widehat y_n)}_*
   &\le d_n,\label{eq:localflow}
\end{align}
on the required tube, with $\Lambda_n,d_n\ge0$.

\textbf{Proposition 1.} If $E_0$ bounds the initial physical error, an outward
recurrence
\begin{equation}
 E_{n+1}\ge\Lambda_nE_n+d_n                                    \label{eq:induction}
\end{equation}
bounds every committed physical error. The candidate selector may be
discontinuous or history-dependent.

\emph{Proof.} Add and subtract the same exact flow at $\widehat y_n$ in
$\widehat y_{n+1}-y_{n+1}$. Apply Eqs.~\eqref{eq:flowstab} and
\eqref{eq:localflow}, then induct along the realized accepted grid. The proof
never evaluates the selector at a second perturbed input, so no selector
derivative or Lipschitz constant is required. \hfill$\square$

Unrolling gives a product-weighted sum of local defects. For an unknown future
adaptive grid those future products are an a posteriori expression, not an
online budget reservation. Prefix accuracy also does not guarantee termination,
adequate remaining resources, or discovery of another admitted candidate.
Failed probes need transactional rollback or an explicit state-change defect;
their work remains charged even when their physical state is discarded.

One concrete RHS-only route to $d_n$ uses a differentiable reconstruction
$v(t)$ and residual $R(t)=v'(t)-f(t,v(t))$. In a fixed Hilbert norm, suppose
\begin{equation}
 \langle f(t,x)-f(t,z),x-z\rangle
 \le\mu(t)\norm{x-z}^2.
\end{equation}
The energy inequality gives $\Lambda_n=\exp(\int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}}\mu)$ and,
for matching reconstruction endpoints,
\begin{equation}
 d_n\le\int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}}
       \exp\!\left(\int_s^{t_{n+1}}\mu(r)\,dr\right)
       \norm{R(s)}\,ds.                                      \label{eq:residualflow}
\end{equation}
The derivative of a polynomial reconstruction is algebraic; it requires no
Jacobian callback. Rigorous integration still requires a valid residual and
domain enclosure, not a few residual samples. Ranocha--Giesselmann give the
relevant one-sided Lipschitz estimate and distinguish reliability of an error
estimator from stability of its step-size controller\cite{RG2024}. Adjoint
methods provide another global-error/conditioning perspective\cite{CP2004},
but their general derivative-dependent adjoint machinery is not assumed to
be available from a derivative-free provider.

\subsection{Two valid anchors and a concrete invalid combination}

Let $\Psi_{n,b}$ be the exact-arithmetic discrete recipe selected for this
step, with its stored coefficients, frozen carrier, chart and norm held fixed.
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\widehat y_{n+1}-\Psi_{n,b}(\widehat y_n)}_*
 \le a_n.                                                   \label{eq:algebraicleaf}
\end{equation}
There are two valid sufficient contracts:
\begin{align}
 \tau_n^{\rm num}&\ge\norm{\Psi_{n,b}(\widehat y_n)
                   -\Phi(t_{n+1},t_n,\widehat y_n)}_*,\nonumber\\
 \text{F:}\quad E_{n+1}&\ge\Lambda_nE_n+a_n+\tau_n^{\rm num},\label{eq:F}\\
 \tau_n^{\rm exact}&\ge\norm{\Psi_{n,b}(y_n)
                    -\Phi(t_{n+1},t_n,y_n)}_* ,\nonumber\\
 \text{D:}\quad E_{n+1}&\ge L_nE_n+a_n+\tau_n^{\rm exact}.    \label{eq:D}
\end{align}
For D, $L_n$ is a discrete-map Lipschitz bound between the exact and numerical
inputs. For F, $\Lambda_n$ is the exact-flow stability factor. The two proofs
insert different intermediate points. In particular, the selected carrier
must not silently be rebuilt at $y_n$ while calling the result the same
function $\Psi_{n,b}$.

Take $\Phi(x)=x/2$, $\Psi(x)=x$, $y_n=0$, and $\widehat y_n=e>0$.
There is no implementation error and the exact-input truncation is zero.
The next physical error is $e$. Combining the flow factor $1/2$ with that
zero truncation would give the false bound $e/2$. D uses $L_n=1$ and is exact;
F includes $\tau_n^{\rm num}=e/2$ and is also exact.

This matters for PR93's cubic planner. Its $1/12$ local estimate and rational
decay sum are evaluated along the \emph{exact} decaying trajectory. Its valid
propagation uses nonexpansiveness of the midpoint discrete map, D with $L_n=1$.
Adding a stronger exact-flow contraction while leaving that truncation term
unchanged is not justified. The counterexample identifies a possible
integration mistake; it does not refute the existing PR93 proof.

\section{Error ownership, physical norms and clocks}

A useful certificate is a path between explicit physical-space anchors:
\begin{equation}
 x^{(0)}=\Phi(t_{n+1},t_n,\widehat y_n),\quad
 x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(r)}=\widehat y_{n+1}.
\end{equation}
If every edge has a bound
$\norm{x^{(j+1)}-x^{(j)}}_*\le\eps_j$, telescoping gives
$d_n\le\sum_j\eps_j$. Model transformation, clock conversion, temporal
method, algebraic solve and endpoint assembly are possible edges. A coarse
certificate covering several consecutive edges is an alternative cover of
that subpath. It is not an additional mandatory charge on top of the same
individual edges.

For example, PR92's represented structural residual already encloses computed
RHS, mean and lift errors. A subsequent residual-to-endpoint map transports
those errors; it need not add them again as though absent. Endpoint accumulation
outside that certificate still requires its own allowance. Conversely, a
single direct physical-flow defect may cover the entire path.

For a reconstruction $\mathcal R$ with Lipschitz bound $K_{\mathcal R}$,
a coordinate discrepancy $\eta$ contributes at most $K_{\mathcal R}\eta$
plus separate reconstruction arithmetic. In the weighted infinity norm
$\norm{x}_w=\max_i|x_i|/w_i$, $w_i>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{x}_\infty\le\max_iw_i\norm{x}_w,
 \qquad
 \norm{x}_v\le\max_i\frac{w_i}{v_i}\norm{x}_w.               \label{eq:norms}
\end{equation}
The constants are sharp by concentrating on a maximizing component. A changed
norm epoch therefore needs the appropriate conversion or a new physical
evaluation. Identical scalar types do not identify physical norms.

Two clock conventions are consistent. Under the physical-label convention,
the canonical time is the exact real value of stored $t_n$, so any difference
between engine $h_n$ and $t_{n+1}-t_n$ belongs to the local defect. Under the
internal-clock convention, exact internal times $\tau_n$ are distinct from
reported labels $t_n$. If $\norm{y'(s)}_*\le M_n$ between the two times,
\begin{equation}
 E_n^{\rm obs}\le E_n^{\rm int}+M_n|t_n-\tau_n|.             \label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}
This observation-only displacement is not reinjected into every later
internal recurrence. For $y'=1$ with exact internal states, the observation
error is the current label gap, not the sum of earlier gaps. For a
nonautonomous shifted vector field, endpoint speed alone does not enclose
the whole shifted step; time regularity and the corresponding sensitivity
or exact-flow estimate are also needed.

\section{Compile complete affine stages in a finite operator algebra}

The next result connects the existing conditioned resolvent to the whole
causal stage target. Let $s$ be the stage count, $s=8$ in the current consumer,
and let $\tau=h\gamma$, $h,\gamma>0$. With the actual represented tableau,
consider the autonomous/frozen-time raw-$U$ equations
\begin{equation}
 WU_i=\tau f\!\left(y+\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}U_j\right)
       +\gamma\sum_{j<i}c_{ij}U_j,
 \qquad W=I-\tau J_0.                                      \label{eq:raw}
\end{equation}
A different mass matrix, stage time or partial-time term requires its matching
equation; Eq.~\eqref{eq:raw} is not changed implicitly.

Declare one whole-space chart and relation in a fixed induced norm:
\begin{equation}
 Z=\tau J_0=cI+\sigma C,\quad \sigma>0,\quad
 \norm{C}\le M,\quad\norm{\pi(C)}\le\rho,\quad\deg\pi=d\ge1. \label{eq:chart}
\end{equation}
The provider identity includes this chart, relation, norm and model epoch.
Sampling a few vector actions does not establish Eq.~\eqref{eq:chart}.
Set $z(x)=c+\sigma x$, $w_0(x)=1-z(x)$ and
$\zeta=(1-c)/\sigma$. If $\pi(\zeta)\ne0$, the existing quotient inverse is
\begin{equation}
 v(x)=\frac{\pi(\zeta)-\pi(x)}
             {\sigma\pi(\zeta)(\zeta-x)},
 \qquad w_0(x)v(x)=1-\frac{\pi(x)}{\pi(\zeta)}.              \label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
It has degree $d-1$. For $\rho=0$, $v(C)=W^{-1}$. More generally,
$\theta_\pi=\rho/|\pi(\zeta)|<1$ yields
\begin{equation}
 \norm{W^{-1}}\le\frac{\norm{v(C)}}{1-\theta_\pi}
 \le\frac{\sum_{k<d}|v_k|M^k}{1-\theta_\pi}.                \label{eq:inversebound}
\end{equation}
The actual stored-polynomial preparation defect must be included in the native
version, as in the existing conditioned implementation. A sensitive denominator
can make this route unfavorable even when the original problem is solvable.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (quotient stage compiler).} Assume $\rho=0$ and the
declared affine identity $f(y+u)=f_0+J_0u$ on the stage domain. Define
$B_{ij}(x)=a_{ij}z(x)+\gamma c_{ij}$ and degree-$<d$ representatives
\begin{equation}
 r_i=\remp_\pi\left[v\left(1+\sum_{j<i}B_{ij}r_j\right)\right].
                                                                    \label{eq:compile}
\end{equation}
Then the unique solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:raw} and its endpoint are
\begin{equation}
 U_i=\tau r_i(C)f_0,\qquad
 y_+=y+\tau p_\beta(C)f_0,\qquad
 p_\beta=\sum_i\beta_i r_i,\quad\deg p_\beta<d.               \label{eq:compiledoutput}
\end{equation}

\emph{Proof.} Equation~\eqref{eq:inverse} makes $v$ the inverse of $w_0$
modulo $\pi$. Evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:compile} at $C$; every multiple of
$\pi(C)$ vanishes. Multiplication by $\tau f_0$ gives the original affine
stage equations. Invertibility of $W$ and strict causality give uniqueness by
stage induction. Applying the endpoint weights gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:compiledoutput}. Normality and diagonalization are not needed.
\hfill$\square$

After scalar preparation, an endpoint-only request uses at most $d-1$
full-space $C$ actions by Horner evaluation, plus vector assembly and a base
RHS if unavailable. Straightforward scalar preparation is
$O(sd^2+s^2d)$ and is reusable at the same $h$/tableau/chart epoch. This is
an operation bound, not measured speed. A requested embedded error or fixed
dense-output linear functional gets its own polynomial. Several outputs can
share one power sequence, but that power basis may have worse rounding than
separate conditioned/Clenshaw evaluations. Complete error and total cost decide.

An endpoint-only implementation must not claim that it produced stage vectors.
Dense/event consumers, stage diagnostics, FSAL or effectful callbacks may require
more information or actual calls. Their contracts constrain the shortcut.
The existing two-channel direct formula can be cheaper than this general
compiler and remains a comparator, as does the shared projected factorization.

\section{Near structure and stored coefficients preserve the original target}

For any actually stored degree-$<d$ polynomials $r_i$, form
\begin{equation}
 s_i=w_0r_i-1-\sum_{j<i}B_{ij}r_j
     =\alpha_i\pi+e_i,\qquad\deg e_i<d.                     \label{eq:respoly}
\end{equation}
Because $\deg s_i\le d$, the quotient $\alpha_i$ is a \emph{scalar}:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_i=-\frac{\sigma}{\pi_d}
     \left(r_{i,d-1}+\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}r_{j,d-1}\right).        \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
For exact compilation $e_i=0$. Outward coefficient arithmetic bounds $e_i$
for stored coefficients, using $E_i(M)=\sum_{k<d}|e_{ik}|M^k$. This avoids
creating an unnecessarily high-degree polynomial merely to certify each
logical stage residual.

Let $\norm{\widehat f_0-f(y)}\le\eps_0$ and define virtual stages
$V_i=\tau r_i(C)\widehat f_0$ and $q_i=\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}V_j$.
Assume a truthful nonlinear remainder bound
\begin{equation}
 \norm{f(y+q_i)-f(y)-J_0q_i}\le\mu_i.                        \label{eq:nonlinrem}
\end{equation}
Their original raw-$U$ residual is exactly
\begin{align}
 R_i=\tau\bigl[& (\alpha_i\pi(C)+e_i(C))\widehat f_0
   +(\widehat f_0-f(y))\nonumber\\
   &-(f(y+q_i)-f(y)-J_0q_i)\bigr],                          \label{eq:fullres}
\end{align}
and hence
\begin{equation}
 \norm{R_i}\le b_i:=|\tau|
  \left[(|\alpha_i|\rho+E_i(M))\norm{\widehat f_0}
        +\eps_0+\mu_i\right].                              \label{eq:resbound}
\end{equation}

Let $\norm{W^{-1}}\le\omega$ and suppose $f$ is $L_i$-Lipschitz on the
segments joining virtual and exact target stage arguments. The existing
strictly causal majorant is
\begin{equation}
 K_{ij}=\omega\bigl(|\tau|L_i|a_{ij}|+|\gamma c_{ij}|\bigr) (j<i),
 \qquad H=\omega\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}K^\ell.                  \label{eq:causal}
\end{equation}
Since $K^s=0$, no global condition $\norm{K}<1$ is required. If the actual
endpoint evaluation differs from $y+\sum_i\beta_iV_i$ by at most
$\eps_{\rm eval}$, subtraction of original stage equations gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\widehat y_+-\Psi_h(y)}
 \le |\beta|^T H b+\eps_{\rm eval}.                         \label{eq:compilerleaf}
\end{equation}
Here $\Psi_h(y)$ is the exact discrete stage target at the \emph{same actual
numerical input}. This is Eq.~\eqref{eq:algebraicleaf}; temporal and clock errors
remain separate under F or D. The proof is componentwise
$\mathrm{error}\le\omega b+K\,\mathrm{error}$ followed by the finite sum.

The evaluation allowance includes output-coefficient assembly, basis conversion,
operator action defects, recurrence arithmetic, multiplication by $\tau$ and
endpoint addition. PR93's backward polynomial defect identities can be used
here. Terms already enclosed in $e_i$ or $\eps_0$ keep their original owner.

Omitted RHS calls require a model-domain witness. A virtual argument radius is
\begin{equation}
 \norm{q_i}\le|\tau|\sum_{j<i}|a_{ij}|
       \left(\sum_{k<d}|r_{jk}|M^k\right)\norm{\widehat f_0}.
\end{equation}
For $f(x)=-kx-gx^3$, $g\ge0$, and declared carrier $J_0=-k$, a radius $R$
gives the remainder bound $g(3|y|^2R+3|y|R^2+R^3)$. This can be too loose,
in which case evaluate the original RHS/residual or take another route.
The Lipschitz domain also has to cover exact target stage arguments; a bound
only on the virtual point does not establish it.

\subsection{Signed transport of correlated structure defects}

For exact coefficients and an affine model, $R_i=\tau\alpha_i\pi(C)f_0$.
The residual blocks share one vector and need not be treated as independent.
If $C$ is self-adjoint in the declared Hilbert norm, with spectrum in a
certified interval $I$ on which $w_0\ne0$, let $L(x)$ be lower triangular
with diagonal $w_0(x)$ and lower entries $-B_{ij}(x)$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\beta_i(V_i-U_i)
 =\tau g_\beta(C)\pi(C)f_0,\qquad
 g_\beta(x)=\beta^TL(x)^{-1}\alpha,                          \label{eq:signed}
\end{equation}
so the endpoint error is at most
$|\tau|\rho\norm{f_0}\sup_{x\in I}|g_\beta(x)|$.
This follows from the affine block error equation and the spectral theorem.
It is an alternative to Eq.~\eqref{eq:compilerleaf}, with stronger assumptions.
A denominator enclosure and signed scalar-envelope preparation must be charged.
For the two-projector model it reduces to the existing two scalar adjoints.

The adverse case $C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\delta&1\\0&-\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$\pi(x)=x^2$, has $\pi(C)=\delta^2I$. Treating that near relation as exact
omits a nonzero original residual. Equation~\eqref{eq:fullres} exposes the
missing term directly. Independently, a nonnormal matrix can have a narrow
eigenvalue set without a useful spectral-theorem bound. Numerical-range
polynomial bounds, when actually certified, can use the established
$1+\sqrt2$ constant\cite{crouzeix}; no such witness is inferred here.

\section{Precision-aware refinement with a derived stopping budget}

Let $r_k=f-Ax_k$, $\norm{I-AP}\le\theta<1$ and $\norm{A}\le a$.
Represent the residual, correction and update by
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat r_k&=r_k+e_k,\quad d_k=P\widehat r_k+z_k,\quad
 x_{k+1}=x_k+d_k+u_k,\\
 \norm{e_k}&\le\eta_k,\quad\norm{z_k}\le\zeta_k,\quad
 \norm{u_k}\le\upsilon_k.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution gives
\begin{align}
 r_{k+1}&=(I-AP)r_k-APe_k-A(z_k+u_k),\label{eq:refineidentity}\\
 R_{k+1}&\le\theta R_k+\kappa_k,\qquad
 \kappa_k=(1+\theta)\eta_k+a(\zeta_k+\upsilon_k).             \label{eq:refinebound}
\end{align}
No commutation between $A$ and $P$ is required. A sharper independent bound on
$\norm{AP}$ may replace $1+\theta$.

For uniform $\kappa_k\le\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
 R_m\le\theta^mR_0+
       \kappa\frac{1-\theta^m}{1-\theta},\qquad
 F=\frac{\kappa}{1-\theta}.                                 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
When $0<\theta<1$ and $R_0>\eps>F$, the least sufficient correction count
for this bound is the least integer $m$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \theta^m\le\frac{\eps-F}{R_0-F}.                           \label{eq:miniter}
\end{equation}
Outward integer verification decides the boundary. If $0<\theta<1$,
$R_0>\eps$ and $\eps\le F$, repeating this uniform bound cannot certify
a finite success. This limits the chosen enclosure, not every algorithm or
representable solution. If $\theta=0$, zero corrections suffice when
$R_0\le\eps$; otherwise one correction is certified when $\kappa\le\eps$,
including equality. If $\kappa>\eps$, repetition of that same uniform bound
does not establish success.

The exact example $\theta=1/4$, $a=1$, $\eta=1/10000$,
$\zeta=\upsilon=1/20000$ has $F=3/10000$. With $R_0=1$ and
$\eps=1/1000$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:miniter} selects six corrections. The registered
check includes a scalar construction attaining the bound for twelve updates,
a noncommuting matrix example, and the $\theta=0$ equality case.

Standalone refinement may use varying certified $P_k$, with the corresponding
product-weighted sum of $\theta_k$ and $\kappa_k$. This does not justify a
changing preconditioner inside an outer algorithm assuming fixed linearity;
freeze that plan or use a separately qualified flexible method. The current
native defaults $\texttt{maximum\_relation\_ratio}=0.5$ and
$\texttt{stagnation\_ratio}=0.95$ are operational policies. The derived rule
can determine mathematical feasibility and the required work without treating
those numbers as theorem constants. Explicit resource caps, failure charging
and original-residual-controlled commit remain.

\section{Allocate the remaining budget without a fixed arbitrary split}

For a fixed request and valid tube, let component $i$ have finitely many
admitted options $j$, errors $e_{ij}\ge0$ and costs $c_{ij}\ge0$ in one
declared unit. Let $W\ge0$ map component errors into required observation
bounds, $r$ contain already-owned errors, and $\epsilon$ be the budgets.
The planner solves
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\min_{j_1,\ldots,j_s}\sum_i c_{ij_i},\\
 &\text{subject to}\quad
 r+W(e_{1j_1},\ldots,e_{sj_s})^T\le\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}                                             \label{eq:menu}
\end{equation}
Extend partial error/cost labels one component at a time. A label can be
removed when another has no greater cost and no greater error in every
observation coordinate, with at least one strict inequality. Equal labels
must retain a representative. Completing a dominated label and its retained
replacement with the same future options preserves dominance, proving that
the least-cost feasible final label solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:menu}.

Shared setup changes this rule. Labels must carry a continuation state that
determines future admissible choices, errors and costs. Prune only within
that state, or under a proved simulation relation. A cold label costing zero
now and 100 later does not dominate a warm label costing one now and zero
later. Equal numerical errors do not make different factorization caches,
coefficient plans or sessions interchangeable.

For weights $(2,1)$ and budget $3/10$, let the error/cost menus be
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A &: (e,c)\in\{(1/10,4),(1/20,7)\},\\
 B &: (e,c)\in\{(1/5,1),(1/10,3),(1/20,6)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The exact optimum has error $3/10$ and cost 7.
An equal physical split requires cost 10. These are declared toy work units,
not native VigilODE measurements.

A continuous initializer is available for a stated work model
$C_i(\eta_i)=a_i\log(B_i/\eta_i)$, $a_i,B_i>0$, with
$\sum_iw_i\eta_i\le B$. At an interior optimum, strict convexity and
stationarity give
\begin{equation}
 \eta_i=\frac{Ba_i}{w_i\sum_j a_j}.                          \label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
For positive bounds $\ell_i\le\eta_i\le u_i$, first check
$B\ge\sum_iw_i\ell_i$. Take all upper bounds if they fit; otherwise use the
clipped multiplier solution in its valid logarithmic range. The actual
integer degree/iteration still needs its original outward test and finite
comparison. A measured cost table makes this optimum conditional on that
table; hardware latency is not a numerical-analysis constant.

An upper arithmetic allowance consuming the entire budget establishes
infeasibility of that decomposition, not a universal precision impossibility.
A true representability obstruction needs a lower distance: on the fixed
lattice $(1/8)\mathbb Z$, the nearest value to $1/3$ is $3/8$, at distance
$1/24$. Tolerance $1/100$ is impossible there. A loose upper allowance of one
for the exactly representable value $1/2$ proves nothing comparable.

\section{Session continuation and complete work conservation}

Three reuse mechanisms require different invalidation rules. Storage capacity
may survive many target changes after initialization. Algebraic objects need
their stated operator, step, chart, norm, model and arithmetic identities.
Proof reuse needs its valid domain and anchor; a theorem can permit semantic
reuse across different input bytes. These mechanisms should not be controlled
by one undifferentiated cache-hit boolean. In particular, keep root workspace
capacity while invalidating Anderson history when its map changes.

The current switching probe extracts an endpoint and proposed step inside
\texttt{with\_session}, then returns from the closure. The native session
ceases to exist. Its existing state already includes workspace, controller,
FSAL, complete output schedule, cursor, cumulative attempt budget and terminal
state. Retaining that object can avoid rebuilding the winner.

\textbf{Proposition 3 (retained-session continuation).} For a deterministic
transition system, pausing an execution at an accepted checkpoint and then
continuing the same full state under the same callback response law and
external controls preserves subsequent states, decisions, outputs and native
callback trace. Work is the sum of the two disjoint trace segments.

\emph{Proof.} The first states after the pause are identical; induction over
future transition inputs gives identical states and emitted events.
\hfill$\square$ This does not follow from copying $(t,y,h)$, and it does not
claim identical host bookkeeping or elapsed time. Provider state affected by
callbacks must be included or isolated; diagnostic counters can remain charged.

The schedule is a concrete integration condition. A session may land only at
registered observations. A winning-capable probe should therefore receive
the complete intended remaining schedule at construction and continue inside
the same closure. Adding a new earlier landing can change the first-window
trace and needs a new comparison. Reaching an endpoint and retaining enough
global attempt budget to continue are separate states.

For requested invocations $A$, admitted entries $E$, pre-entry refusals $R$,
completed entries $C$, failed entries $F$, and pending entries $P$,
\begin{equation}
 A=E+R,\qquad E=C+F+P.                                      \label{eq:accounting}
\end{equation}
All scheduled workers must be joined/accounted before setting $P=0$.
These are not physical callback identities; each entered callback/action has
its own ledger. Thus Q-FAIR-AB's attempted-versus-admitted distinction and
PR92's entered-versus-charged physical-work repair are different obligations.

A completed admissible competitor of cost $C$ dominates an unfinished trial
whose proved final-cost lower bound is already $L\ge C$, when ties prefer
the completed result. For integer costs strict improvement is equivalent to
final cost at most $C-1$. A fixed nonnegative linear work functional supplies
a monotone accumulated lower bound. This is a same-window statement and
does not predict unexecuted fatal callbacks or future-regime costs. RHS counts,
instructions and elapsed seconds remain different units. PR93's residence
theorem still requires a future variation certificate; session reuse does
not supply that missing assumption.

\section{Implementation decisions and unresolved theory}

The immediate implementation sequence is to qualify the cumulative existing
code, then measure N01 fused action and N02 planner changes independently,
followed by N04 retained-session continuation. The quotient compiler is a
focused N03/N05 extension on declared low-degree models. Its remainder and
domain providers precede general near-affine omission. N06 residence retains
its future-variation obligation.

Allocation, scratch reuse, output fusion and complete-session lifetime can be
improved through code while preserving their stated mathematical targets.
Outward preparation and defect kernels require implementation-level arithmetic
proofs and native checks. General nonlinear carrier order, stiffness-uniform
accuracy, physical branch control, arbitrary domain certification and future
cost variation require additional theory. No local failed candidate establishes
a universal impossibility of the project's original construction.

The inherited references preserve these distinctions. Algebraic-geometry
regularization and nonlinear root certification\cite{robust,nonlinear} do not
by themselves select the original physical branch or provide a derivative-free
implementation. The DAE symmetry construction has its stated reduction and
linear-system scope\cite{dae}. Geometric RK/composition identities\cite{eth}
still require separate treatment of adaptive selection and inexact stages.

The integrated design therefore selects among eligible structures and targets,
transports each admitted local result through a correctly anchored physical
contract, and charges every attempted computation. Proof-derived constants
determine admissibility and precision feasibility. Declared resource budgets
and measured service costs determine the economic choice within that region.
